FAQ

What add-in gives me Excel shortcuts in Google Sheets?

The main options are Chrome extensions: SheetSpeed (free Core), SheetWhiz (free plan, Pro $9.99/mo), ShortieCuts (14-day trial, then $7.99/mo) and Spex. XLKeys is currently unavailable (checked Oct 18, 2026). Google Sheets also has a free built-in toggle: Help → Keyboard shortcuts → Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts. Strictly, these are Chrome extensions rather than Google Workspace add-ons, because only an extension can capture Alt key sequences.

Which Excel shortcut extension for Google Sheets is free?

Google's compatible shortcuts toggle is free. SheetSpeed Core is free forever and includes formula tracing (trace precedents). SheetWhiz has a free plan for formatting shortcuts. ShortieCuts is a 14-day free trial.

Which one has the most users?

SheetWhiz, with 10,000 users and a 4.6-star rating on the Chrome Web Store on Oct 18, 2026. ShortieCuts shows 1,000 users. SheetSpeed and Spex are newer listings.

Which extension supports Google Docs and Slides too?

ShortieCuts covers Sheets, Slides and Docs. SheetSpeed and SheetWhiz cover Sheets and Slides. Spex covers Sheets only.

Do Excel Alt shortcuts like Alt E S V work in Google Sheets?

Not natively. Google's toggle adds many Ctrl shortcuts but no Alt ribbon sequences. Extensions such as SheetSpeed, SheetWhiz, ShortieCuts and Spex add them. On a Mac, they use Option in place of Alt.

Can I trace precedents in Google Sheets?

Not natively. SheetSpeed traces precedents for free (Ctrl+Shift+[), and SheetWhiz and ShortieCuts include tracing in their paid plans.

Is SheetSpeed the same as sheetspeed.com?

No. SheetSpeed, the Excel-shortcuts Chrome extension, lives at sheetspeed.agi-jarvis.com and on the Chrome Web Store (id ljlmgielacploapelnjhodicgbjipcaa). sheetspeed.com is unrelated.
