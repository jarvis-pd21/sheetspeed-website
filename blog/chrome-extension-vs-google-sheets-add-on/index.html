Chrome Extension vs Google Sheets Add-on: Which Do You Need for Excel Shortcuts?

Excel calls them add-ins. Google has two different things. Here's the difference.

People switching from Excel often search for an "add-in for Google Sheets." Google has two kinds of tools, and for keyboard shortcuts the difference matters.

Google Workspace add-onChrome extension
Where you get itWorkspace Marketplace (Extensions → Add-ons)Chrome Web Store
Where it runsInside the file, in a menu or sidebarIn your browser, on the Sheets page
Works in any browserYesChrome and most Chromium browsers
Can catch keystrokes like Alt, E, S, VNot reallyYes
Typical permissionsAccess to the spreadsheet via Google APIsBrowser permissions on the Sheets site

For Excel shortcuts: a Chrome extension

Keyboard shortcuts need to catch your keys as you type, which is what Chrome extensions can do. That's why SheetSpeed, ShortieCuts, SheetWhiz and Spex are all Chrome extensions (as was XLKeys, which is currently unavailable (checked Oct 18, 2026)).

When an add-on makes sense

Sidebar tools that don't depend on the keyboard, such as data connectors, mail merge and some goal seek tools, work well as add-ons.
