How to Enable Excel Shortcuts in Google Sheets (2026)

The built-in Google Sheets setting, step by step, what it can't do, and the extensions that fill the gaps, with prices. Written for people switching from Excel.

The short answer

  1. Turn on Google's built-in setting (free, 30 seconds): Help → Keyboard shortcuts → Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts.
  2. Add one Chrome extension for what that setting can't do: press-and-release Alt sequences (Alt, E, S, V), trace precedents, goal seek.

Use both together. The rest of this guide shows exactly what each layer covers, where it falls short, and how to choose an extension. We make SheetSpeed, one of the extensions below; we've tried to describe every option fairly and link to each vendor's own page so you can check.

Step 1: Turn on "Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts"

  1. Open any spreadsheet at sheets.google.com in a desktop browser.
  2. Press Ctrl+/ (Windows/ChromeOS) or ⌘+/ (Mac). Or click Help → Keyboard shortcuts.
  3. At the bottom of the dialog, switch on Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts. The toggle turns green.
  4. Close the dialog and try Ctrl+Shift+= on a selected row to insert a row.

Google documents this setting on its Keyboard shortcuts for Google Sheets help page ("Use other spreadsheet shortcuts"). It's a per-account preference, so you may need to turn it on again on a new computer or profile.

FAQ

How do I enable Excel shortcuts in Google Sheets?

Open a spreadsheet, press Ctrl+/ (⌘+/ on Mac) or click Help → Keyboard shortcuts, and turn on "Enable compatible spreadsheet shortcuts" at the bottom of the dialog. For Excel's press-and-release Alt sequences and trace precedents, add a Chrome extension such as SheetSpeed, SheetWhiz, ShortieCuts, XLKeys or Spex.

Does Alt, E, S, V work in Google Sheets?

Not on its own. Google's paste-values shortcut is Ctrl+Shift+V. Extensions like SheetSpeed (free Core), ShortieCuts and XLKeys add Alt, E, S, V as a press-and-release sequence.

Why don't Alt shortcuts work like Excel even after turning on compatible shortcuts?

With Google's setting, Alt-based shortcuts generally have to be held together with the next key, while Excel lets you press and release Alt and then type the letters. Sheets has no ribbon, so press-and-release Alt sequences need an extension.

Is there a free way to get Excel shortcuts in Google Sheets?

Yes. Google's compatible shortcuts setting is free, and SheetSpeed Core is free on the Chrome Web Store, including Alt E S V paste special, Alt H B borders and trace precedents (Ctrl+Shift+[).

Can I trace precedents in Google Sheets?

Google Sheets has no built-in trace precedents. SheetSpeed traces precedents of the active formula in its free Core (Ctrl+Shift+[). SheetWhiz, XLKeys and ShortieCuts offer tracing in their paid plans.

Do these work on a Mac?

Yes in Chrome on macOS. Extensions use Option in place of Alt. Some of Google's compatible shortcuts behave differently or are unavailable on Mac, so check Help → Keyboard shortcuts on your machine.
